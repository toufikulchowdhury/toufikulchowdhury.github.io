I hold an MSc in Data Science from the University of Greenwich (2025), where my dissertation analysed NHS England workforce statistics for midwives and ambulance staff from 2009 to 2023 and forecast regional trends with SARIMAX models. It found that the mix of staff groups differs markedly between regions: in 2023 managers outnumbered ambulance staff both nationally and in London.

I am currently preparing a manuscript on quality-adjusted access to maternity care in England. I work with national administrative data, combining travel-time and accessibility modelling, spatial statistics and machine learning at small-area scale.

Research

  • Spatial Health
    Quality-adjusted spatial accessibility to maternity care in England: A provider-determined measure and the limits of neighbourhood-scale inference
    Md Toufikul Islam Chowdhury and Jia Wang
    Manuscript in preparation

    A measure of spatial accessibility to maternity care that accounts for the rated quality of providers, estimated for 33,744 neighbourhoods using road-network travel times, regulator ratings and birth registrations, and analysed alongside maternity records for 106 commissioners and midwifery staffing for 42 health systems.

  • Health Workforce
    Spatiotemporal workforce dynamics and forecasting in NHS England: Spotlight on midwives and ambulance staff
    Md Toufikul Islam Chowdhury
    MSc dissertation, University of Greenwich, 2024–25 · Supervisor: Dr Jia Wang · Mark: 80%

    An analysis of NHS England workforce statistics for midwives and ambulance staff, 2009 to 2023, by region, integrated care system and organisation, with SARIMAX forecasts to 2028. Midwifery headcount rose from 23,554 to 27,078 and support to ambulance staff from 14,159 to 28,458; in 2023 there were more managers (26,769) than ambulance staff (20,677).

Feb 2025 Took part in the CUSP London Data Dive 2025 at King's College London, a team challenge on urban waste management with 80+ students from eight universities. We linked recycling, fly-tipping, deprivation, council tax and housing data for London's 32 boroughs and the City of London, and I produced the interactive borough map in Python. Recycling rates were lower in more deprived boroughs (Spearman ρ = −0.63) and in boroughs with more purpose-built flats (ρ = −0.64).
