Research

  • Spatial Health
    Near but capacity-constrained: distance, capacity and urban pressure in access to neonatal intensive care in England
    Md Toufikul Islam Chowdhury and Jia Wang
    Manuscript in preparation, for submission to Urban Science (MDPI)

    Access to neonatal intensive care is usually described by distance alone. For 568,188 births in 33,609 small areas of England (July 2023 to June 2024), we estimated car travel times on a road network carrying observed 2024 speeds and modelled intensive-care cot availability relative to births. Half of births were within 24.5 minutes of a neonatal intensive care unit, yet 17.5% were near one but in areas of below-median modelled capacity. Among births near a unit, child income deprivation was associated with this capacity constraint between unit catchments but not within them.

  • Health Workforce
    Spatiotemporal workforce dynamics and forecasting in NHS England: Spotlight on midwives and ambulance staff
    Md Toufikul Islam Chowdhury
    MSc dissertation, University of Greenwich, 2024–25 · Supervisor: Dr Jia Wang · Mark: 80%

    Fifteen years of monthly NHS workforce statistics (2009–2024) for the 42 integrated care systems, with staffing per 100,000 population mapped against deprivation: several deprived areas had fewer midwives and ambulance staff. Staffing was forecast to 2029 with ARIMA, SARIMAX and double exponential smoothing, compared by rolling-origin evaluation, and presented in a prototype interactive dashboard.
