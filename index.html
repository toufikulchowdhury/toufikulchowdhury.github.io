Research

  • Manuscript
    Quality-adjusted spatial accessibility to maternity care in England: A provider-determined measure and the limits of neighbourhood-scale inference

    A three-tier study of quality-adjusted access to maternity care across England's 33,744 neighbourhoods, 106 commissioners and 42 health systems, linking road-network travel times, regulator quality ratings and administrative birth records. The study combines network-based accessibility modelling, spatial autocorrelation analysis (global and local bivariate Moran's I under catchment-restricted permutation), cluster-robust regression with effective-sample-size correction, and gradient-boosted machine learning evaluated under spatially blocked cross-validation. Once provider quality is taken into account, the share of births beyond thirty minutes rises from 1.76% to 25.19%.

  • MSc dissertation
    Spatiotemporal workforce dynamics and forecasting in NHS England: Spotlight on midwives and ambulance staff
    University of Greenwich, 2024 · Supervisor: Dr Jia Wang · Mark: 80%

    A statistical analysis of NHS England workforce data for midwives and ambulance staff from 2009 to 2023, at regional, integrated care system and organisational level, using interactive geospatial maps, staff-density heatmaps and SARIMAX time-series forecasting. Midwife numbers rose by only around 3,500 over the period (23,554 to 27,078), while support to ambulance staff doubled (14,159 to 28,458). By 2023, managers outnumbered ambulance staff nationally (26,769 against 20,677).

  • Team challenge
    CUSP London Data Dive 2025, King's College London
    February 2025 · 80+ students from eight universities

    Working in a team on the challenge "How does urban waste management contribute to cities' sustainability goals towards 2030 and 2050?", we linked recycling rates, fly-tipping incidents, deprivation (IMD), council-tax bands and housing type for London's 33 boroughs. I built an interactive borough-level map of fly-tipping and recycling in Python (GeoPandas, Folium). Across boroughs, recycling rates were lower where deprivation was higher (Spearman ρ = −0.63) and where more households lived in purpose-built flats (ρ = −0.64).
