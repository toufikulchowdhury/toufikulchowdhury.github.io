My research interests centre on the quantitative study of healthcare systems and population inequalities, particularly where access, workforce capacity and health outcomes vary geographically. I work primarily with large-scale administrative and population data, using statistical, spatial and computational methods to examine these differences.

I am interested in pursuing doctoral research in statistics and related quantitative disciplines, particularly in spatial and spatio-temporal methods, statistical learning, and applications to health and population research.

Research

Spatial inequalities in maternity healthcare accessibility

Current research · England

My current research examines geographical inequalities in access to maternity care across England. The work integrates small-area healthcare, demographic and socioeconomic data to investigate how accessibility varies between populations and places, and how these patterns relate to wider dimensions of inequality.

NHS England workforce trends and forecasting

MSc research · University of Greenwich

My MSc research investigated NHS England workforce dynamics, with a particular focus on midwifery and ambulance services. The study examined temporal, regional and organisational variation in the workforce and applied time-series methods to model longer-term workforce change.

Research interests

My broader interests include spatial and spatio-temporal statistics, statistical modelling, quantitative health research, population inequalities, healthcare accessibility, and statistical learning.
